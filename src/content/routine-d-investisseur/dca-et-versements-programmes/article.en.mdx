DCA (dollar-cost averaging) means investing a fixed amount at a regular interval, rather than trying to pick the "right time" to invest a large sum all at once.

## The mechanism

Every month, the same amount gets invested, regardless of where the market stands. When prices are high, that fixed amount buys fewer shares. When prices are low, it buys more. The average purchase price naturally smooths out over time, without any timing decision ever being made.

<Example title="A worked example">
€100 invested every month for 4 months in an ETF whose price moves like this: €50, €40, €45, €55.

Month 1: €100 / €50 = 2 units. Month 2: €100 / €40 = 2.5 units. Month 3: €100 / €45 ≈ 2.22 units. Month 4: €100 / €55 ≈ 1.82 units.

Total invested: €400, for about 8.54 units, an average price of about €46.8 per unit, lower than the simple average of the four prices (€47.5). The mechanism mechanically buys more when it's cheaper.
</Example>

## Why automation beats conviction

DCA's real advantage isn't primarily mathematical, it's behavioral. An automated contribution depends on no mood, no anxiety-inducing headline, no temptation to "wait until things calm down" before investing, which in practice delays the decision indefinitely. By removing the repeated decision, you also remove the chance to get the timing wrong out of overconfidence or fear.

<Callout>
Investing a large sum all at once (lump sum) statistically beats DCA on average over the long run, because markets go up more often than they go down. DCA isn't optimal in the strict sense: it's a deliberate trade-off between theoretical performance and discipline that's actually sustainable over time, for a recurring contribution (salary, monthly savings) rather than a lump sum available all at once.
</Callout>

## Putting it into practice

Most online brokers offer a scheduled-investment feature that automatically debits a chosen amount on a fixed date each month, into one or more predefined lines. Once set up, there's no monthly decision left to make: the only remaining active step is the periodic review (see the article on behavioral biases for the recommended frequency).

<KeyTakeaway label="Key takeaway">
DCA isn't a magic formula or the mathematically optimal strategy: it's a discipline tool that removes timing decisions from the equation, for an investor putting in recurring savings rather than a one-off lump sum. Set up once, it runs without effort or repeated decisions.
</KeyTakeaway>
