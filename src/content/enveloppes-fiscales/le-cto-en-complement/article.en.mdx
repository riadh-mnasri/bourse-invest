The ordinary brokerage account (CTO) doesn't have the favorable tax treatment of the PEA, but it fills exactly what the PEA can't do. It's not a duplicate, it's a complement.

## What the CTO allows that the PEA doesn't

No geographic restriction: US stocks, Asian stocks, anywhere in the world, held directly, without going through a synthetic replication structure. No contribution cap. No holding-period constraint.

It's the natural wrapper for:
- Holding a specific US stock directly (Apple, Nvidia...) rather than through a synthetic ETF.
- Accessing physical-replication ETFs on indices not eligible for the PEA, if you'd rather avoid synthetic counterparty risk on part of your exposure.
- Continuing to invest beyond the PEA's €150,000 cap.

## Taxation shapes the behavior you should adopt

Every gain realized on a CTO is immediately subject to the 30% flat tax (12.8% income tax + 17.2% social contributions), with no deferral mechanism like the PEA has. Concretely: selling and rebuying the same position a few months later, even to rebalance, triggers immediate taxation on the realized gain.

<Callout>
This tax mechanic naturally pushes toward buy-and-hold rather than active trading. This isn't a stylistic preference, it's a direct consequence of the tax regime: every round trip has an immediate tax cost that the PEA doesn't have.
</Callout>

## Brokerage fees: often negligible today

Several online brokers (including mobile-first ones) offer near-zero order fees on European stocks and ETFs, which makes the CTO usable even for small, regular contributions, without fees meaningfully eating into performance.

<Example title="A concrete role in a strategy">
An investor who has filled most of their global index exposure through their PEA can use their CTO for a more targeted pocket: a few individually tracked US stocks, as a small share of the total portfolio. The CTO's less favorable tax treatment is accepted in exchange for access to those securities, and a buy-and-hold behavior limits the tax impact over time.
</Example>

<KeyTakeaway label="Key takeaway">
The CTO isn't an alternative to the PEA, it's its complement for everything the PEA doesn't cover geographically or beyond its cap. Its less favorable tax treatment naturally makes it a wrapper to hold over time rather than trade actively.
</KeyTakeaway>
